About the data

How the graph behind this site was made, what its labels mean, and the public sources for every statement about AFSA, the courts and Hume.

Everything in the graph is synthetic

Every person, company, address, phone number, wallet and record was generated by a script. Names are built from syllables, phone numbers and bank details are masked, wallet addresses are random, and lenders are labelled by category, such as "Card lender 03". No AFSA data was used or seen.

How it was generated

What the line styles mean

Every relationship records how it is known. Declared: stated on a form, a register entry, or a response to a notice. Matched: linked by entity resolution, such as an invoice's email domain matched to the debtor's. Observed: recorded on a public blockchain. Algorithm results, such as communities and rankings, are inferences.

A connection is a lead, not a finding

A path between two people shows that records connect them. It does not show why. Decisions about people need a trustee's or investigator's judgement, and procedural fairness.

Real use needs legal authority

Connecting insolvency, PPSR, company and exchange records depends on the legal authority to collect and share each one, privacy safeguards, and a record of who looked at whose information.

The test has stated limits

The results on the Why graph page come from data and methods we designed. That page lists the limits in full.

What each use case runs on

Use cases 1 and 2 use the contact phone and email recorded on debt agreement proposal forms, agreement outcomes recorded on the NPII, AFSA's registers, complaints and past investigation records. Use case 3 depends on data a regulator does not hold in one place today, and says so on its page.
