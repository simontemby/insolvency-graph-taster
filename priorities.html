AFSA's priorities

AFSA's 2026–27 Regulatory Action Statement names three priority harms. This page summarises them from public sources. Use cases 1 and 2 address the second harm with data a regulator already receives. Use case 3 addresses the first, and depends on data it does not hold in one place today.

Manipulation of personal insolvency proposals

AFSA describes the use of personal insolvency proposals to conceal wealth. Its stated focus includes inadequate investigations of a debtor's affairs and non-compliant conduct at creditors' meetings.

Source: Accounting Times, 10 Aug 2026, reporting the Regulatory Action Statement

Harmful debt agreement practices

AFSA names unaffordable agreements, unrealistic budgets, poor or incomplete advice to debtors, and excessive or poorly structured fees charged by registered debt agreement administrators.

Source: Accounting Times, 10 Aug 2026

Misuse of the PPSR

AFSA names registrations that are disingenuous, fraudulent, frivolous or vexatious, and expired or invalid registrations that remain on the register and can restrict access to credit.

Source: Accounting Times, 10 Aug 2026

Personal insolvency agreements are few: 210 in 2024–25. Each is decided by a vote of creditors, so a small number of related or friendly creditors can change the result. The PPSR is the opposite case: millions of registrations, where the problem is finding the few that are misused.

The courts have acted on manipulated votes. In Inspector-General in Bankruptcy v Hartnett (February 2025), the Federal Court set aside a personal insolvency agreement where the debtor had arranged invoices in his own name, those purported creditors voted for the agreement, and a major voting creditor was a related party.

Source: Lavan, case summary

Where a graph applies

In each harm, the evidence that matters is spread across separate records: forms lodged with AFSA, the company register, the PPSR, bank and exchange records. A rules check compares one record with another. A graph holds them as one connected structure, so a question like "is this voting creditor connected to the debtor, by any route up to five relationships long?" becomes a single query.

That is the claim the rest of this site tests, on synthetic data, with its limits stated.
